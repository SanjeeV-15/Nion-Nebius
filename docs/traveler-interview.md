# Talking to travelers: a short, neutral question list

Purpose: find out whether the problem is real for other people, in their own words.
This is research for you; what you learn and how you describe it is yours to write.

**How to run it**
- 3 to 5 people who travel. Ten minutes each, in person or on a call.
- Ask about their *last* trip, not what they *would* do. People are bad at predicting.
- Don't mention Nion until the end. Don't explain, defend or sell. Say "tell me more".
- Write down their exact words, not your summary. Ask before recording anyone.

**Questions (in this order)**
1. Think of your last trip somewhere unfamiliar. Walk me through the first hour after you arrived.
2. What did you look up on your phone during that time? How did you do it?
3. Was there a moment when using your phone was awkward, annoying or slow? What was happening?
4. Did you ever forget, lose or have to re-find something (a place, a price, a name, an address)? What was it?
5. How did you keep track of what you spent, or what you still wanted to do? What worked and what didn't?
6. Have you tried talking to a voice assistant while travelling? What happened?
7. What would have to be true for you to trust something like that with your trip? What would worry you?

**After the answers, show it (optional)**
Let them try Nion for two minutes with no instructions. Watch; don't help. Then ask:
"What did you expect it to do that it didn't?" and "Would you use this on your next trip? Why or why not?"

more convincing to judges than a big claim you can't back up.
