Nion — scope

A hands-free voice companion for people on the move: you talk, it talks back like a person on a phone call, so you can get answers and think things through without looking at a screen.

The unique kernel

It feels like another person on the other end of a call, not a search box. It knows roughly where you are, uses your name, talks back, asks follow-ups, and can think a decision through with you.

How it fits together

One loop, repeated: ask out loud, hear the answer, follow up. If the demo shows that smoothly, the proof of concept works.

Who it's for

A traveler moving through an airport with hands full and earphones in, rushing to a gate or just landed in a new city.

They have random questions: where to eat nearby, what time it is back home, how to get from the airport downtown. Today they stop, pull out the phone, and type.

Same idea could help anyone who wants less screen time, but travelers come first.

Why it matters

"A lot of times, I can't just go every time, look at my mobile, type in the answer, read it out."

Helpful when traveling, and when eyes hurt and you want to rest but keep working. The goal: a wide audience, and a first version that is narrow and effective, not overkill.

What "working" looks like

A short demo on a phone with earphones. Click each moment.

1 Places to eat, ranked by price
You say: "Hey Nion, what are some places to eat around me? Rank them by price."
Nion says: names places with prices and cuisine, then asks if you want to narrow it down.
2 Time in another city
You say: "What's the time in Denver?"
Nion says: "It's 11:59 pm in Denver."
3 Getting around
You say: "How do I get from Denver International Airport to Union Station?"
Nion says: a few options, spoken aloud.
4 Thinking a decision through
You say: "I got a cake last time and it wasn't good. I'm thinking of getting this one instead. What do you think?"
Nion says: an opinion, plus a follow-up question back to you.
The "oh, that's cool" beat: nobody touches the screen, and Nion sounds like a person who knows where you are.

The boundary

In The proof of concept
  • Voice in and voice out: you speak, Nion replies aloud
  • Activated by voice ("Hey Nion"); exactly how is a spec decision
  • Knows roughly where you are, for local questions
  • General knowledge, time in other cities, local suggestions (places to eat, getting around)
  • Back-and-forth conversation, including thinking through a decision
  • Addresses you by name
  • One device: a phone with earphones
Later Worth doing, just not now
  • Checking messages and email ("you have three unread, want to know who from?")
  • Reading out and managing a to-do list, recurring tasks, reminders
  • Booking and doing things on your behalf
  • Watch and other devices
Cut Named, with a reason
  • A full to-do list or task manager: the first version is conversation, and you want to avoid overkill.
